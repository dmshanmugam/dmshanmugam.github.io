Hi! My name is Divya. I am a National Library of Medicine Fellow at Columbia's Department of Biomedical Informatics.

I develop machine learning methods that use large-scale healthcare datasets to understand how people seek, receive, and deliver care, and to develop more reliable AI systems. I am interested in how human behavior shapes the data we use to train, update, and evaluate machine learning systems and what that means for their use in clinical settings. My recent work develops methods to <span class="research-emphasis">audit training data</span> ([NWH 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04133); [JAMA HF 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.0491)); to <span class="research-emphasis">evaluate models</span> when labels are limited or unreliable ([CHI 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581482); [NeurIPS 2025](/pdfs/Neurips_2025_SSME_final.pdf)); and to <span class="research-emphasis">improve the reliability</span> of pretrained systems at test time ([ICCV 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11156); [CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22764)).

I am always excited to work with students and collaborators on problems where real clinical settings raise new technical questions. Previously, I was a postdoc at Cornell Tech, advised by Emma Pierson and Jenna Wiens. Before that, I earned my Ph.D. at MIT, where I was lucky to work with John Guttag.
